This paper investigates how different methodological choices affect publication-based rankings of computer science departments. Through an ablation study, we show that publication venue quality has the largest impact on ranking outcomes, while factors such as recency, co-authorship handling, and subarea clustering contribute less. The resulting rankings achieve a strong correlation (Pearson’s r > 0.90) with U.S. News peer assessments, highlighting the alignment between venue-based metrics and perceived research strength.
